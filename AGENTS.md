# Repository Instructions

## Writing Style

These writing rules apply to all website content, including blog posts, research pages, teaching pages, notes, project pages, and other user-facing prose.

- Write in plain, direct prose that is easy to read.
- Follow the spirit of *The Elements of Style* and *On Writing*: prefer clarity, brevity, concrete wording, and active voice.
- Avoid inflated academic filler, generic transitions, and repetitive structure.
- Do not write in a way that sounds obviously generated by AI, an LLM, or an agent.
- Make the writing sound like a careful human researcher, engineer, or educator explaining ideas to another human reader.

## SEO

These SEO rules apply to all website content, including blog posts, research pages, teaching pages, notes, and project pages.

- Every new page must include a clear `title` and a concise `description` in front matter.
- Articles and notes should include `categories` that reflect the topic accurately.
- Where a page has a meaningful preview image, include `image` and `image-alt` metadata.
- Keep titles specific and readable. Do not use vague or generic titles.
- Keep descriptions factual, concrete, and useful for search snippets. Avoid filler and keyword stuffing.
- Write opening paragraphs that clearly state the topic so search engines and human readers can identify the page quickly.
- Preserve crawlable internal links where relevant, especially between related notes, posts, research pages, and project pages.
- Do not remove or override the site-wide canonical URL, Open Graph, Twitter Card, favicon, or site image settings unless there is a clear reason.

## Notes

When adding or updating files under `notes/`:

- Include the original source URL near the top of the note.
- Include the original subject tags from the source near the top of the note.
- For arXiv papers, include the canonical `arXiv` abstract URL and the original arXiv subject categories.
- Notes should summarize essential points and preserve details that are useful for future research, rather than copying the full source text.
